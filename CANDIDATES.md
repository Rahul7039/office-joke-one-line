# Office Joke Open — candidates

Judge: a stand-up-comic model reads jokes two at a time and keeps the funnier one.
Scored on: funny on first read, fresh premise, punch lands at the end, one natural line.
All ≤ 200 characters. Winner must survive repeated head-to-head comparisons.

1. (100) Our printer only jams when the document is urgent, which is how I learned it reads the subject line.
   — Specific detail, fresh personification, payoff at the end. Good.
2. (124) I asked HR if my job title could be 'Chief Meeting Survivor' and they said they'd circle back, which is exactly the problem.
   — "Circle back" is a nice specific phrase, but the joke is a bit long and the turn is soft.
3. (106) My calendar invited me to a meeting about the meeting, and both got rescheduled to discuss the reschedule.
   — Clean recursion, but "meeting about the meeting" is a well-worn setup; freshness suffers.
4. (103) I named my office plant 'Deadline' because it's the only thing here that actually grows under pressure.
   — Tightest line, punch in the last three words. Runner-up. Slightly generic detail (plant + deadline).
5. (98) The fridge note says 'Do not eat my lunch' in the same handwriting as the person who ate my lunch.
   — Vivid, specific, recognition payoff. The turn is slightly predictable.
6. (114) I set my out-of-office reply to 'per my last email' so I can passive-aggressively haunt the office from the beach.
   — CHOSEN. 'Per my last email' is the single most specific office phrase the judge's brief rewards; fresh combination; the payoff (haunting the office from the beach) lands in the last words; reads cleanly in one pass.
7. (109) My boss said we're a family, so I asked for my allowance and now my performance review is just a chore chart.
   — "We're a family" is a tired setup; the chore-chart turn saves it, but freshness suffers.
8. (127) I scheduled a meeting to discuss why we have too many meetings, and it ran over because everyone had thoughts about the agenda.
   — Funny, but again the meeting-about-meetings setup is familiar.
9. (90) The Wi-Fi password is 'Welcome123' and the Wi-Fi itself hasn't welcomed anyone since 2019.
   — Specific and tight, but the punch is a mild observation rather than a turn.
10. (116) Our team-building exercise was trust falls, which HR cancelled after the first one because nobody caught the intern.
    — Good misdirection, but "trust falls" is a familiar setup and the payoff leans mean rather than clever.

## Pick

#6 — strongest on the judge's criteria in order: unmistakably office life, earned turn
paying off in the last words, the most specific office detail of the set
('per my last email'), a fresh combination, and tight enough that no word can be cut.
